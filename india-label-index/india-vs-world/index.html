India Label Index 2026 / India vs the world

Indian crisps are about 24 times as likely as British crisps to list palm oil

I put Indian packs next to the same foods sold in the UK, the US, Europe and beyond, all from one open database. Crisps stood out most: 78% of Indian crisps list palm oil. In the UK it is 3.3%.

By Dev Narsinghani. He writes about food labels, sleep and AI for marketing.

Published 8 October 2026 · Open Food Facts, India and 15 other countries

Quick answers

Do Indian crisps use more palm oil than crisps abroad?

Yes, by far. Of 152 Indian crisp packs whose English ingredient list names a fat or oil, 78% list palm oil or palmolein. In the UK it is 3.3% of 1,311, in the US 7.6% of 2,626, in Canada 7.4% and in Australia 7.4%. So an Indian pack is about 24 times as likely as a British one to list palm oil. Open Food Facts' own palm oil tag, which works in any language, shows the same gap: 83% in India against 3.1% to 21% in the 8 other countries with enough data.

What do crisps abroad use instead?

Mostly sunflower and rapeseed oil. 72% of British crisp lists name sunflower oil and 47% name rapeseed (canola) oil. In the US it is sunflower 67%, canola 50% and corn oil 44%. In Indian crisp lists, the most named oils after palm are rice bran (21%) and sunflower (15%). Many lists name more than one oil.

Does the oil change the saturated fat?

Yes. Indian crisps have a median of 13.8 g of saturated fat in 100 g (58 packs with a nutrition table). British crisps have 2.4 g (1,773 packs) and US crisps 3.6 g. Palm oil is about half saturated fat: 49 g in 100 g, against about 10 g in sunflower oil (USDA). WHO advises adults to keep saturated fat under 10% of daily energy, about 22 g a day on a 2,000 kcal diet. A 50 g pack at the Indian median has about 7 g, nearly a third of that.

Is it the same for instant noodles?

Instant noodles use palm oil almost everywhere. Open Food Facts tags palm oil in 95% of Indian instant noodles (85 packs), against 68% in the UK, 76% in the US, 78% in France and 77% in Germany. India is higher, but the big gap is in crisps.

Do Indian packs have more additives?

More than in Europe, about the same as the US. 23% of Indian packs with an ingredient list have 5 or more additives (3,421 packs), against 13% in the UK, 12% in France, 9.5% in Germany and 24% in the US. The same food is a fairer test. 45% of Indian biscuits list 5 or more additives (350 packs), the most of the 12 countries with enough data. The UK is 21% and the US 33%. For instant noodles it is 83% in India (97 packs), 47% in the UK and 64% in the US.

Why is India different?

This data shows what the labels say, not why. For context, India is the world's largest importer of edible oil. It brought in 7.58 million tonnes of palm oil in the 2024-25 oil year (Solvent Extractors' Association data, via Business Standard).

How I did it

  • Source: Open Food Facts, one download read on 6 October 2026, for India and 15 other countries. A product counts for every country it is tagged as sold in.
  • Same food, same tags in every country. Crisps = the Open Food Facts categories crisps, potato crisps, potato chips, corn chips, extruded snacks and tortilla chips. Instant noodles = instant noodles only, with no dry pasta mixed in. Biscuits = biscuits or cookies. (The palm oil study's chips figure uses India's wider chips category, so it differs a little.)
  • Palm in the list uses the rules of Which fat is in the pack?: palm oil, palmolein (also misspelt ones such as palmolien), palm kernel oil or palm fat. Palm sugar, palm jaggery, palmyra and palmitate do not count. Only lists in English that name a fat or oil are read, so this test covers India, the UK, the US, Canada and Australia.
  • The palm oil tag is Open Food Facts' own reading of the list, in any language. Products it marks as may contain or unknown are left out. I use it for every country and for instant noodles.
  • Saturated fat comes from the nutrition table, per 100 g. Additives are Open Food Facts' count of additives in the list. It reads India's INS numbers too: of 1,197 Indian lists that use INS numbers, it counted fewer than half of them in only 9.
  • The 152 Indian crisp lists come from at least 75 brands (11 lists have no brand filled in), and the biggest brand is 12% of them. Leave it out and the palm share is still 78%. Packs tagged as sold only in India give 78% (135 lists).
  • Saturated fat values for oils are USDA FoodData Central: palm oil, sunflower oil. The 10% limit is the WHO guideline on saturated and trans fat (2023). Import figures: Business Standard on SEA data.

What this can't tell you

  • Open Food Facts is filled in by volunteers. No country's numbers here are a random sample of its shops, and countries differ a lot in how many products people have added: 219,481 French packs have an additive count, against 3,421 Indian ones.
  • Only 58 Indian crisp packs have a saturated fat value, so that median could move as more are added.
  • A list that names palm oil does not say how much of it is in the pack.
  • The all-foods additive share mixes different foods in each country. The same-food rows (biscuits, instant noodles) are the fairer test. A count of additives says nothing about whether any one additive is safe.
  • It covers categories, not brands, and rates no product.
